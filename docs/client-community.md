# Client Community

Community is available to signed-in clients on iOS and mobile web. Joining creates a nickname, selected fitness avatar, and rotatable 16-character invite code. Clients can change their nickname and avatar later. A code sends a pending request; the recipient accepts or declines it. Either participant can remove an accepted connection. Leaving Community removes the profile, invitations, connections, shared achievement summary, and props tied to those connections.

Sharing XP, earned badges, completed workout count, and gym visit count are separate choices and start off. Accepted connections can read each enabled field. A separate `public_progress_opt_in` choice also starts off. When enabled, the same selected milestones appear to every signed-in client under nickname and avatar only. The public feed does not return a user ID, invite code, real name, email, or raw records. Turning off public visibility removes the member from the feed. Invite codes remain for private connections. A member may choose a nickname that resembles their real name; the app does not reveal account names automatically. The `progress_opt_in` setting from the earlier preview remains stored but has no sharing behavior.

Accepted connections can give one prop per recipient per UTC day and see total props received. Props have no text field and are removed with the connection. Connected clients can also send a workout or gym visit challenge with a target from 1 to 7. The recipient must accept before the seven-day period begins. The two participants see each other's completed workout or check-in counts during that period; counts come from server records, not uploaded achievement summaries. A connection removal removes its challenges. Neither props nor challenges expose workout logs, body measurements, health data, email addresses, gym locations, or progress photos.

The shared summary is calculated from each client's complete workout history and uploaded from the iOS or web app when Community is opened or achievements refresh. It is a client-supplied display projection; it must not be used as the authoritative source for future rankings or awards. A future leaderboard needs server-calculated scores from the underlying records.

The database contract is in the `client_community_connections`, `community_selective_progress_sharing`, `community_avatar_nickname_props`, `community_public_progress`, and `private_community_challenges` migrations. It uses row-level policies for profiles, accepted connections, sharing preferences, and achievement summaries. Public feed and challenge RPCs use narrow functions in the unexposed `community_private` schema. Their public wrappers are callable only by authenticated clients; the functions check participant relationships and consent.

Leaderboard remains a future feature. Client-supplied XP must not be used for authoritative rankings.
